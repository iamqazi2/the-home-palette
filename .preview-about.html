Chapter One — The Beginning

A Palette Built in Pakistan, for Pakistani Tables

We started The Home Palette in 2024 with one dinner set, a small studio in Bahria Town and a long list of potteries to visit. What we found was extraordinary: family workshops turning out ceramics as good as anything imported — and almost none of it reaching the homes it was made for.

So we became the bridge. We buy directly from the workshops, agree the quality standard together, and bring the pieces to you under one palette of colours that genuinely work with each other. No catalogue of a thousand mismatched items; a considered range you can build on over years.

Today that range covers ten collections — ceramics, glass, woven jute, wood and metalware — and sets tables in homes, cafés, restaurants and hotels across Pakistan.

A table should look like the people who set it — warm, colourful, and entirely their own.

  • Sourced at the source

    We buy straight from the potteries and weaving families we work with — no importers, no middle layer, no mystery about who made your plate.

  • Designed in colour families

    Fourteen glazes, chosen to sit beside one another. A bowl bought a year later still belongs with the set you already own.

  • Checked piece by piece

Chapter Two — What We Believe

A Home Is Made in the Details

At The Home Palette, we believe that a beautiful home is created through the little details — the pieces you choose, the way you style them, and the moments you create around them.

We are a home and tableware brand bringing together thoughtfully crafted ceramics, woven pieces, metalware, cutlery and wooden accessories, designed to add warmth, character and personality to your spaces.

But for us, it’s about more than just beautiful products. We’re passionate about supporting local women artisans and celebrating the craftsmanship behind every piece. From hand-finished ceramics to beautifully woven placemats, runners, tissue boxes and accessories, each piece represents skill, creativity, and the hands that bring it to life.

We believe beautiful tableware should also be made for real living. That’s why we focus on pieces that are safe, durable, functional and made to be enjoyed every day — whether it’s a quiet breakfast at home, a dinner with loved ones, or a table set for a special celebration.

Today, The Home Palette is loved not only by individuals and families, but also by hotels and restaurants across Pakistan, who choose our pieces to bring a distinctive touch to their spaces and tables.

From everyday homes to hospitality spaces, we’re proud to be a part of the places where people gather, connect, celebrate and create memories.

Because we don’t just want to decorate your tableWe want to make every tablescape a moment.

Chapter Three — Inside the Workshop

How a Piece Becomes Part of the Palette

Six stages, most of them done by hand. It is slower than machine production — and it is the reason no two reactive-glaze pieces ever leave the kiln identical.

Greenware on the workshop racks

01
  1. 01

    Clay & sourcing

    Every batch begins at the workshop, with clay chosen in person rather than ordered from a catalogue. We work with a small number of family-run potteries and buy whole production runs instead of cherry-picking, which is what lets those workshops plan months ahead instead of week to week.

    Family-run partners
  2. 02

    Shaping & casting

    Plates and bowls are thrown on the wheel or slip-cast in plaster moulds, then left to firm up to leather-hard before the foot is trimmed. It is the slowest stage of the whole process, and the one that decides whether a stack of six plates will sit flush on your shelf.

    24–48 hours drying
  3. 03

    The first firing

    Dried greenware goes into its bisque firing and comes out porous and chalky — strong enough to handle, thirsty enough to take glaze evenly. Anything that warped or cracked is pulled here, before a single rupee of glaze is spent on it.

    ≈900°C
  4. 04

    Glaze & colour matching

    This is where the palette is actually made. Glazes are mixed in batches and checked against the master tile for each colour — Sage Green, Limone Yellow, Reactive Cloud Grey — so the range stays consistent season after season. Reactive glazes are the deliberate exception: they are allowed to move in the kiln, which is why no two pieces come out identical.

    14 colour families
  5. 05

    Into the kiln

    The glost firing takes each piece well past 1,100°C, where the glaze vitrifies into a hard, food-safe surface that survives daily use and the dishwasher. Roughly twelve hours up and twelve hours back down — rush the cooling and the glaze crazes.

    1,100°C and beyond
  6. 06

    Inspection & packing

    Every piece is turned over, tapped for a clean ring, checked for pinholes and measured against the rest of its set. What passes is wrapped in foam sleeves, corner-blocked and boxed in double-wall cartons — which is why breakage is the rarest email in our inbox.

    Piece by piece

Pieces that do not pass inspection stay at the workshop. We would rather be short of stock for a week than send out a second as though it were a first.

Chapter Five — The Journey

From One Dinner Set to Ten Collections

A short history of the palette — and where it is headed next.

  1. 2024

    The first collection

    The Home Palette opens with a single dinner set, a studio in Paradise Commercial, Bahria Town Phase 4, and a long list of potteries worth visiting.

  2. 2025

    Colour becomes the system

    Glazes are standardised into named colour families so pieces bought months apart still match — and Shop by Colour goes live on the site.

  3. 2025

    The Weave Collection

    Hand-woven jute placemats, coasters and baskets join the ceramics — our first non-ceramic line, and still one of the most reordered.

  4. 2025

    Hospitality begins

    Cafés, restaurants and hotels start ordering by the crate. Theme consultation and tailored packages follow soon after.

  5. 2026

    Wood and metal complete the table

    The Wooden Line and THP Metalware arrive, taking the palette past ten collections — from the plate to the platter to the serving spoon.

  6. Today

    One palette, a thousand tables

    Ten collections, fourteen colours and deliveries to every major city in Pakistan — with the next collection already on the wheel.
